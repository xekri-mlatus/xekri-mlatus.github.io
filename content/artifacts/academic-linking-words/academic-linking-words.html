Common confusions

Pairs and placements that most often go wrong in student writing.

01however vs but.

However is an adverb, not a conjunction. ✗ The test was significant, however the effect was small. ✓ …significant; however, the effect… or a new sentence.

02in contrast vs on the contrary.

In contrast compares two things. On the contrary rejects the previous statement. ✗ Urban areas grew; on the contrary, rural areas shrank.

03on the other hand.

Introduces the second side of a balanced comparison, ideally prepared by on the one hand. It is not a general substitute for however.

04whereas vs while.

Both contrast clauses. While can also mean "during", so whereas is safer when ambiguity is possible.

05therefore / thus / hence.

All three can be adverbials with a comma. Only hence can come straight before a noun phrase (hence the need for…), and thus can come before -ing (thus reducing…).

06thereby vs whereby.

Thereby + -ing = "by this means" (…, thereby reducing noise). Whereby introduces a relative clause = "by which" (a process whereby cells…).

07due to vs owing to.

Due to is safest after a form of be (the delay was due to…). At the start of a sentence, prefer owing to or because of.

08despite vs although.

Despite + noun phrase or -ing; although + clause. ✗ Despite the sample was small…

09e.g. vs i.e.

e.g. = for example (an incomplete list); i.e. = that is (an exact restatement).

10albeit.

Takes a phrase, not a full clause: ✓ albeit with a smaller effect; ✗ albeit the effect was smaller.

11respectively.

Only when two lists match item by item, and usually at the end of the clause.
