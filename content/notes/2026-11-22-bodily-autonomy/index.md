What concerns me with regard to the LGBT rights discussion in the context of minority rights in general and migration is the focus on identity as opposed to bodily autonomy rights.

It seems to me that LGBT rights don't require references to one's personal identity, because they can be justified by the right to make decisions about one's own body, for the most part if not completely. Every human being has a right to have sex and romantic relationships in the way they want with a partner or partners they prefer (or not to engage in such activities). Everyone has a right to freely express their gender through appearance, behavior and bodily modifications without discrimination. Of course, common disclaimers about not violating others' rights apply as usual.

It shouldn't matter whether having a specific sexual orientation or being a transgender person is natural, social or caused by any other reason or a combination of these, or whether it is an identity or not. The question is and always has been: "Are others allowed to prevent us from living the life we desire?" And for me, the answer is no, because we own our bodies as everyone else does. By the way, it's the same reason why abortion must be allowed.

Moreover, bodily autonomy must be above all identity issues, including religion. And I feel that LGBT people don't have to associate with or support religious groups or societies whose ethics condemn us, despite what many modern Western left-wing politicians and activists say. We should support LGBT people who suffer in such societies, and even help them with immigration to friendly countries. I also welcome those who fight oppressive traditional values and institutions, as well as apostates and atheists from such countries. However, for those who reject bodily autonomy on the basis of their culture, the doors must stay shut.
